How hillsides change a plumbing call

Lafayette's elevation is about 320 feet, and the city runs from the Berkeley Hills side toward the grassy hills east of them. On a slope, the sewer lateral may run steeply down to a street below the house, or it may have to climb to meet a main above it. Both cases matter for backups.

A house below street level can take sewage from the public main if a cleanout is not fitted with a backwater device. A sewer camera inspection shows how the lateral runs, and sewer line cleaning handles the blockage.

Houses above the street

  • Gravity helps the lateral
  • Long downhill runs can collect roots
  • Cleanouts may be far from the house

Houses below the street

  • Backups reach the lowest drain first
  • A backwater valve can protect the house
  • Pumps may be used in some homes

Tankless and tank heaters on slopes

Garages may be tight and set against retaining walls. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock. See water heater repair.

Water pressure on steep streets

Pressure differs between the top and bottom of a hill, so a regulator or an expansion tank may be needed.

Mature trees and roots

Oak woodlands and mixed woods grow near many lots, and roots track moisture into laterals.

Weekday commuters

Lafayette grew from 7,114 people in 1960 to 20,484 in 1970 as a commuter town. Same-day appointments help when everyone is away at work.

When the rain comes: winter drainage and backups

Rainy months bring more stormwater into the ground around laterals, and saturated soil can shift pipes and push roots toward joints. If a drain gurgles during or after heavy rain, treat it as a warning and call early. Our drain cleaning page explains how we find whether the problem is local or in the lateral.

Lafayette ZIP code we serve

Lafayette uses one ZIP, 94549, covering the area from the BART station and the Lafayette Reservoir to the hills toward Briones. The same code is the page's primary ZIP, and one 24/7 line answers for every address.

West Lafayette and the early 1950s

Henry J. Kaiser built an estate in West Lafayette in the early 1950s, and the town shifted from farms to a commuter suburb around the same period. Homes from that era share the postwar mix of galvanized lines and clay or cast iron drains. If you own one, our repiping page explains when a full replacement is better than another repair.

Work in Lafayette follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), with Central San's specifications applying to sewer laterals in its service area.
