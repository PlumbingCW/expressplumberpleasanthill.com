When a faucet leak is an emergency

A faucet leaking one drop per second wastes thousands of gallons a year, and the drip signals a worn part that will eventually fail completely. If the leak is under the sink, it can swell the cabinet floor and start mold.

Turn the oval valves under the sink clockwise to stop the water, then call. If the valves themselves are stuck or leaking, we replace them.

Drip from the spout

Usually the cartridge, washer or seat. The faucet no longer seals when off.

Leak at the handle

Worn O-rings or packing nut. Water creeps up the stem.

Low flow

Mineral buildup in the aerator or cartridge. Ask your water utility for its water quality report, since we have not verified hardness at your address.

Leak under the sink

A loose or corroded supply line, a failed shutoff valve, or a faucet body leak.

Repair or replace

If the faucet is relatively new and a cartridge is available, repair is the better value. If the finish is worn off, the body is corroded, or parts are discontinued, replacement is more sensible.

A replacement is also the moment to fix the supply lines and shutoff valves beneath, which often cost little extra while the sink is open. For the garbage disposal under the same sink, our disposal repair page covers that side.

What a repair visit looks like

1

Shut off and inspect

We close the supply valves, remove the handle and look at the cartridge, seat and O-rings.

2

Match the parts

We identify the make and model so the replacement part fits, or we recommend replacement if parts are gone.

3

Rebuild or replace

We install the new cartridge or faucet and replace worn supply lines at the same time.

4

Test and clean up

We run hot and cold, check under the sink for drips and leave the cabinet dry.

Outdoor faucets and hose bibs

Hose bibs fail when a hose is left attached and water pressure stays on the pipe. A dripping hose bib wastes water in a flowerbed and can rot the wall behind it. We replace the packing or the bib, and we can add a vacuum breaker if code requires one.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

  • Close both valves under the sink, turning them clockwise by hand only.
  • If a valve will not turn, do not force it. Close the main instead.
  • Empty the cabinet and put a towel or pan under the leak.
  • Do not use the faucet again until it is repaired.
