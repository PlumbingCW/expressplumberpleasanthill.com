High Water Bill? Common Hidden Leak Causes in Pleasant Hill, CA

A higher water bill with no change in habits is one of the most common reasons people discover a leak. The bill is the symptom. The cause is usually in the toilet, the irrigation system or a buried line.

This guide walks through the likely causes in order of how often we find them, and what you can do before calling.

By the Pleasant Hill Express Plumbing team · Updated November 12, 2026

Start by comparing bills

Pull your last year of bills, if you have them, and compare the same months. Summer bills are higher because of irrigation. A rise in winter, when you use less water outdoors, is a stronger clue. Note the billing period too, because some bills cover longer cycles.

Then read your meter. If it moves with every tap off, the leak is real. If it does not, the extra use may be an appliance, a guest, or a billing change.

Toilets and irrigation account for most of the leaks homeowners can fix themselves. Underground lines and slab leaks need a locator, and our leak detection page describes the process.

Do a one-hour meter test

1

Shut everything off

No taps, appliances, irrigation or ice makers.

2

Record the meter

Write down the number and take a photo.

3

Wait one hour

Do not use any water.

4

Read it again

Any change is a leak that needs tracing.

What to do if the meter moves

Close the shutoff valve at the house. If the meter stops, the leak is inside. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house. The second case is a service line repair and is the owner's cost.

If you find the leak, call your provider and ask about a leak adjustment. Many providers have a process for billing after a documented leak, and they will want proof of repair. Keep the plumber's invoice.

Why leaks are hard to see

Water goes to the lowest point

It runs along pipes and joists before it stains anything.

Slabs hide leaks

Water can soak into soil under the slab for weeks.

Irrigation leaks stay underground

Only a wet patch or a high bill shows them.

Small leaks add up

A steady drip can waste thousands of gallons across a billing cycle.

Prevent the next surprise

  • Check your meter once a month.
  • Replace toilet flappers every few years.
  • Walk the irrigation system each season.
  • Know where your main shutoff is.
  • Replace old galvanized pipe before it fails. See our repiping page.

Reading your bill like a plumber

Most bills list usage in units, and a unit is a fixed volume your provider defines on the bill. Compare units, not dollars, since rates and fees change. A rise in units with no change in household habits is the number that tells you to hunt for a leak.

Look at the daily average if your bill shows one. A leak runs around the clock, so it raises the daily average even in a month when the household was away. If the daily average is high in a week when no one was home, the water was going somewhere.

Why meter tests are the standard first step

Water providers and plumbing guidelines alike use the same basic idea: if the meter moves while every fixture is off, water is escaping. That is why the one-hour test above comes before any digging. It costs nothing, it needs no tools, and it tells you whether to keep looking in the house or move outside.

If the leak turns out to be a toilet, replacing the flapper under the manufacturer's guidelines is the usual fix. If it is a buried line, the repair must meet the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Pleasant Hill: who reads your meter

Contra Costa Water District serves part of Pleasant Hill, and other providers serve other addresses, so the first step is to find your provider on your bill. Ask the provider about leak adjustment policies, since they differ and we cannot speak for any agency.

Many Pleasant Hill homes date from the postwar decades, and galvanized steel from that era corrodes inside and can pinhole. If your leak is in old pipe, it is rarely the last one, so ask for a pressure test.
