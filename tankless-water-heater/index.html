Tankless Water Heater Service in Pleasant Hill, CA

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows instead of storing it, so it only runs when a tap is open. In Pleasant Hill we install, repair and maintain these units, and we explain the gas, venting and flushing needs before you commit.

My tankless heater is lukewarm or showing a code

Callers usually start with a symptom, not the word tankless: hot water that never gets hot, a unit that clicks and shuts off, or a code on the display. Those problems often trace back to scale on the heat exchanger, a clogged inlet filter, a dirty flame sensor or an undersized gas line.

We read the code, check the filter and test gas pressure before touching the unit. Many fixes are small, and we tell you plainly if the unit needs a bigger repair.

Flush and descale: the service people skip

Minerals in tap water collect inside the heat exchanger as water is heated. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit to dissolve that buildup and restore flow. Manufacturers typically call for periodic flushing, and your manual gives the interval.

We do not state a local hardness figure here because we have not verified one for your address. Test your tap, or ask your water utility for its annual water quality report. Your bill names whether Contra Costa Water District or another supplier serves you.

Is tankless right for your home?

Good fit when

  • Gas line can be upsized
  • Vent path is easy
  • Household hot water use is steady
  • You want to free floor space

Think twice when

  • Gas supply is limited
  • Venting would need major work
  • Simultaneous demand is very high
  • You want the lowest upfront cost

Simultaneous demand is the question to ask. Two showers and a dishwasher at once can exceed a single unit's flow, so we size by fixtures. If you are comparing options, see our replacement page for tank alternatives and the installation page for planning steps.

Installation requirements

  • A gas line sized for the unit's input rating, which is usually larger than a tank's.
  • A sealed vent and intake, installed to the manufacturer's clearances.
  • A condensate drain on high-efficiency models.
  • A shutoff valve and service valves so the unit can be flushed.
  • An electrical supply for the controls and fan.

California Plumbing Code governs the work, and permit requirements vary by city. Ask the Pleasant Hill building division which permit applies.
