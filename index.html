Local knowledge

Plumbing in a postwar Contra Costa town

Pleasant Hill grew quickly after the war. The post office opened in 1948 and the Gregory Gardens subdivision was developed in 1950. The plumbing in many homes from that period reflects the materials of the time: galvanized steel supply pipe, clay or cast iron drains and tank water heaters tucked into garages. Those systems often work for decades and then fail all at once.

That history shapes the calls we get along Contra Costa Boulevard and the streets around Pleasant Hill Park, Soldiers Memorial Park and the Iron Horse Regional Trail. Pinhole leaks and slab leaks in aging supply lines, root intrusion in older sewer laterals and water heaters past their prime are the repeat stories. Our leak detection and sewer inspection pages explain how we find the cause before we recommend a repair.

Roots deserve special mention. Central San, the district that serves Pleasant Hill, calls root intrusion the leading cause of sewer backups and says the property owner is responsible for the whole private lateral. It advises professional inspection or cleaning at least every other year when trees stand near the line. If you have not had a camera look at yours, our sewer line cleaning page explains the next step.

Guidelines on this site come from the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and from the published rules of Central San and the City of Martinez.

Pleasant Hill sits between Interstate 680 and State Route 242, close to the Pleasant Hill/Contra Costa Centre BART station and Diablo Valley College. That position puts Concord, Walnut Creek and Martinez roughly ten to fifteen minutes away, depending on traffic and dispatch.
