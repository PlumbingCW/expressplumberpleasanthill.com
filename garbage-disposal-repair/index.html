Repair or replace

Repair when

  • The unit is a jam or a loose connection
  • The motor tests healthy
  • A seal or flange can be replaced
  • The unit is fairly new

Replace when

  • The housing leaks
  • The motor is burned out
  • The unit is very old and noisy
  • You want more grinding power

A leaking housing cannot be patched, so replacement is the only fix. Leaks at the sink flange or the drain elbow can often be sealed.

What should and should not go down the disposal

  • Run cold water before, during and after grinding.
  • Avoid grease, oil and fat, which coat the line downstream.
  • Avoid coffee grounds in large amounts, fibrous peels and bones.
  • Never put a hand or utensil into the chamber while the unit has power.
  • Cut large scraps into small pieces.

Grease is the main reason a disposal-equipped kitchen line clogs. Many Pleasant Hill kitchens share a single run among the sink, dishwasher and disposal, so a downstream blockage can look like a disposal failure.

Installing or replacing a disposal

Most replacements reuse the sink flange and mounting ring. The new unit hangs from the existing mount, the drain elbow reconnects and the unit is wired to its cord or plugged in. Mismatched mounting kits are the usual snag, and we carry common kits so the job finishes in one visit.

Check that the new unit's horsepower suits your household. A small unit in a busy kitchen jams more often. A larger unit grinds more finely and strains the drain less. Our toilet repair page covers the other fixture that shares the stack.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

  • Switch the disposal off at the wall and unplug it if you can reach the cord.
  • Never put a hand into the chamber, even when the unit is off.
  • Do not run the dishwasher into a disposal that is leaking or jammed.
  • Empty the cabinet and place a pan under any drip.
