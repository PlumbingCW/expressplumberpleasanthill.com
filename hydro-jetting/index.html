Emergency Hydro Jetting in Pleasant Hill, CA

When a cable keeps punching a hole through a clog that closes up again in a week, the next step is hydro jetting, and the Pleasant Hill line is answered 24/7. If your main line is backing up right now, stop using water and call. A jetting visit is often possible the same day when a crew is free.

Hydro jetting is cleaning the inside of a drain or sewer pipe with a high-pressure stream of water from a specialized hose and nozzle, which strips away grease, scale, silt and root hairs that a cable leaves behind.

When jetting is an emergency, and when it is not

A main line that is fully blocked is urgent: wastewater has nowhere to go and will rise in the lowest drain in the house. In that case we usually open the line with a cable first, then jet to clean the pipe so the blockage does not return the next week.

A line that drains slowly but still drains can be scheduled. The call is still worth making early, because a partial blockage tends to become a full one at the worst moment, like a holiday dinner or a full house.

What jetting removes

Grease

Cooking fat sets up inside kitchen lines and holds food debris. Jetting melts and flushes it, which is why restaurant kitchens jet on a schedule.

Scale

Mineral deposits narrow old iron pipe. The jet knocks the loose layer off without removing sound metal.

Roots

Fine roots enter at joints and grow into a mat. A jet with a cutting nozzle clears them, though they regrow unless the joint is repaired.

Silt and sand

Flat spots in old clay or Orangeburg pipe collect debris. Jetting flushes it out, and the camera tells us whether the pipe is sagging.

We run a camera before jetting when we suspect a break, because high-pressure water can make a cracked pipe worse. If the camera shows a collapse, we stop and talk through sewer line repair before going further.

Why Pleasant Hill lines need it

Central San, the sanitary district serving Pleasant Hill, names root intrusion as the leading cause of sewer backups. Trees along residential streets send roots toward the moisture in joints, and clay pipe laid in the postwar boom has many joints.

The district recommends a professional inspection or cleaning at least every other year when trees are near the lateral. Jetting is the cleaning half of that advice, and our sewer inspection page covers the camera half.

Safety and limits

Jetting is not suitable for every pipe. Brittle cast iron with advanced corrosion, collapsed clay and some bellied lines can be damaged by pressure. We choose the pressure and nozzle to fit the pipe, and we tell you when a different approach is safer.

For kitchen and restaurant grease lines, jetting often pairs with sewer line cleaning on a recurring schedule.

What to do before the crew arrives

  • Stop running water so the line does not fill further.
  • Clear a path to the cleanout, and move vehicles and planters away from it.
  • Keep children and pets away from the cleanout and any standing water.
  • Note which fixtures are slow so the crew knows where to look.
