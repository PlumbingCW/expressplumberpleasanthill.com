What College Park is

College Park is a residential neighborhood in Pleasant Hill. A local guide describes it as a suburban community with Interstate 680 on its east side and a Diablo Valley College campus inside it. Public data lists streets such as Taylor Boulevard, Viking Drive, Ruth Drive and Golf Club Road, with Grayson Creek and the Contra Costa Canal among nearby features.

Parks in and around the neighborhood include Paso Nogal Park, Shadowood Park and Frank Salfingere Park, and a private golf course sits among some of the subdivisions.

A mix of homes and a mix of owners and renters

Public profiles show mostly detached houses, with townhomes and other attached units mixed in. Two profiles disagree on the typical age of the housing, so the plain reading is that the neighborhood is not one age.

If you own a house

You decide on repairs. Close the main valve in a leak and call. We explain the likely cause and price before work starts.

If you own a townhome or condo

Check your governing documents. Shared lines are usually the association's, and in-unit parts are usually yours.

If you rent

Tell your landlord or manager first, unless water is actively flowing. Close the shutoff if you can, then call the landlord.

If you lease space nearby

Your lease says who pays. Ask before you hire a plumber, and keep photos of any damage.

Calls we expect here

Water heaters get attention in homes of any age, so water heater repair and tankless water heater questions are common. Slow drains lead to drain cleaning. For a leak whose source you cannot find, leak detection is the starting point, and a failed supply line may need burst pipe repair fast.

Sewer and permits

Sewer repairs in Pleasant Hill fall under Central San's permit and inspection rules, and the owner is responsible for the lateral to the main. For other plumbing work, ask the City of Pleasant Hill building division whether a permit applies.

What to have ready when you call

  • Whether you own, rent or lease, since that decides who approves the repair.
  • Whether the problem is inside your unit or in a shared line, if you live in attached housing.
  • The location of the main shutoff, and whether it has been closed.
  • A photo of any water damage, taken before anything is moved or cleaned.

Once we know who decides and where the water is coming from, we describe the likely cause and price first. If a landlord or association must approve the work, we explain what to send them so the repair is not delayed.
