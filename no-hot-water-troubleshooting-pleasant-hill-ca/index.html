No Hot Water? A Troubleshooting Guide for Pleasant Hill, CA Homes

When the shower runs cold, there are only a handful of likely causes, and a few of them you can check in ten minutes. This guide walks through safe checks for gas and electric tanks and tankless units, and tells you when to stop and call.

Never relight a pilot when you smell gas or find water around the heater. In either case, stop and call.

If you are not sure what you have, look at the label on the unit. It lists the fuel, the capacity and the model number.

Safe checks for a gas tank

1

Check the gas control

Make sure the knob is on and not set to pilot.

2

Look at the pilot

If the flame is out, follow the label's relight steps.

3

Smell for gas

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.

4

Try once, then call

If the pilot will not stay lit, the thermocouple or gas valve may be bad.

Do not repeat relighting attempts. A pilot that will not stay lit is telling you a part has failed. Our water heater repair page explains the diagnosis.

Safe checks for an electric tank

  • Check the breaker. A tripped breaker is the easiest fix.
  • Press the red reset button on the upper thermostat panel, with power off first.
  • Check that the thermostat is set above the lowest setting.
  • Do not open panels with power on. Call us if the breaker trips again.

A repeat trip means a shorted element or wiring problem. Resetting it again and again is a fire risk.

Tankless units

A tankless heater shows a code on its display. Look it up in the manual. Many codes point to a dirty inlet filter, low gas pressure, a blocked vent or scale. Cleaning the inlet screen is something many homeowners can do. Descaling and gas work are not. Our tankless page covers the maintenance side.

Lukewarm or runs out fast

Sediment in the tank

A flush can restore capacity if the tank is sound.

Broken dip tube

Cold water mixes into the hot supply at the top of the tank.

Failed lower element

On electric tanks, only the upper element works, so you get a short supply.

Mixing valve or crossover

A fixture or valve may be mixing hot and cold.

Stop and call when you see these

  • Water around the base of the tank.
  • A gas smell.
  • Rumbling or popping from the tank.
  • Rusty water from only the hot taps.
  • A relief valve that discharges.

A leaking tank cannot be repaired. Our replacement page covers the same-day path.

Temperature settings and scald safety

A tank thermostat set too low gives lukewarm water, while one set too high is a scald risk, particularly for children and older adults. Many manufacturers suggest a moderate setting, and the label on your unit gives its recommendation. Check it before you raise the setting.

If you adjust a gas control, wait several hours before judging the result, because a tank takes time to recover. If water is still lukewarm the next day, the issue is not the dial, and it is time to call.

Why manufacturer guidelines come first

Every water heater label lists the maker's instructions for relighting, resetting and setting the temperature. Those guidelines override anything you read online, including this page, because they are written for your exact model. If the label is missing or unreadable, the model number on the rating plate lets you find the manual.

Installation standards, including the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), set the safety devices around the tank. If any of them are missing, mention it when you call.

Pleasant Hill: code items and your supplier

The City of Martinez publishes a water heater handout that cites California Plumbing Code items such as seismic straps, a relief valve drain ending 6 to 24 inches above the ground and a gas shutoff valve beside the unit. If your heater lacks them, a replacement is a good time to add them.

Contra Costa Water District serves part of Pleasant Hill, and your bill names your provider. If you see mineral crust on fixtures, ask your provider for its water quality report instead of guessing at hardness.
