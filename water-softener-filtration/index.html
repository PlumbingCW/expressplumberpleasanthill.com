Water Softener and Filtration in Pleasant Hill, CA

Chalky buildup on fixtures, spotty glasses or a heater that gets noisy can point to mineral content in water. In Pleasant Hill we test first, then recommend a softener, a filter or nothing at all, depending on what your water actually shows.

Water softening removes hardness minerals such as calcium and magnesium, and filtration removes particles, chlorine taste or specific contaminants, using different equipment for each purpose.

Start with a test, not a sales pitch

We do not quote a hardness figure for your address, because we have not verified one. Your water utility publishes an annual water quality report, and a test kit or a lab test gives your own tap number. Your water bill names your supplier.

Softeners and filters solve different problems. A softener targets hardness. A carbon filter targets taste and chlorine. A sediment filter catches particles. Buying the wrong one wastes money.

Whole-house or point of use

Whole-house system

  • Treats every tap
  • Protects the water heater and pipes
  • Higher upfront cost
  • Needs a drain and electrical outlet nearby

Under-sink filter

  • Treats drinking water only
  • Lower cost
  • Easy to install
  • Does not reduce scale in the heater

Scale shortens the life of a tank or tankless heater. Our tankless page explains flushing for units in hard water.

Installation details

  • A bypass valve so you can shut the system off for service.
  • A drain connection with an air gap for regeneration water.
  • An electrical outlet for the control head.
  • A location that keeps the unit accessible for salt or cartridge changes.
  • Pressure and flow checked after installation.

California limits how some softeners may discharge brine in certain areas, and local rules vary. Ask your sanitary district or city about restrictions. Central San serves Pleasant Hill, and we advise checking with the agency that serves your address.

Signs that point to a water treatment problem

Chalky white buildup

Crust on faucets, shower heads and kettles usually means minerals. Test your tap before you decide.

Spotty dishes and glasses

Mineral spots after the dishwasher run can be a rinse issue as well as hardness.

Chlorine taste or smell

A carbon filter at the sink often fixes taste without any softening.

Brown or cloudy water

Rust particles or sediment need a sediment filter, and persistent discoloration may mean corroding pipe.

If the water looks brown right after work on a main, run a cold tap for several minutes. If it does not clear, call us, since the cause may be pipe corrosion and a filter would only hide it.
