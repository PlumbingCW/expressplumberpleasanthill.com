Commercial emergencies we respond to

Backups and overflows

A floor drain, restroom or kitchen line backing up into a work area.

Water heater failure

No hot water in a kitchen or salon, which can force a closure.

Leaks and floods

A burst pipe in a ceiling or a service line leak outside.

Restroom failures

Running toilets, clogged urinals and leaking flush valves in public restrooms.

The first steps match a home: stop water use in the affected area, find the shutoff, and photograph the damage. Call us and say whether the business can stay open. We prioritize getting the essentials working.

Restaurants and food service

Kitchens produce grease, and a grease line that is not cleaned on a schedule will back up. We clear the line with a cable or hydro jetting and suggest a maintenance schedule based on what we find.

Floor drains in a kitchen are a common pain point. They carry grease and food, and they back up at the wrong time. We also check the disposal, the three-compartment sink and the dish machine connections.

What to tell us on the first call

  • The type of business and whether you can stay open.
  • Which fixtures or areas are affected, and whether the customers can still use the restroom.
  • Whether the water or the gas is shut off and where the shutoffs are.
  • Who has authority to approve work and who will meet the plumber.
  • Whether the building is owned or leased, since the landlord may control the shutoff or the lateral.

Tenants often ask who pays. That depends on the lease, so check it, and call the property manager when the failure may be in a shared line. We keep photos and a written summary of what we found, which helps with the landlord and with your insurer.

Planned maintenance and installation

A scheduled visit costs less than an emergency. We offer routine drain cleaning, water heater checks, restroom fixture replacement and camera checks of laterals. Because the owner is responsible for the lateral under the street per Central San, a periodic camera inspection is a practical safeguard.

For new tenant improvements, our plumbing installation page covers the rough-in and fixture steps. Commercial work follows the California Plumbing Code, and permit requirements depend on the city.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

  • Close the shutoff for the affected fixture, or the main if water is spreading.
  • Move stock, paper and electronics away from the water.
  • Put up a sign to keep staff and customers out of the wet area.
  • Do not use the restroom or the dish machine on a backed-up line.
  • Photograph the problem before cleanup for your records.
